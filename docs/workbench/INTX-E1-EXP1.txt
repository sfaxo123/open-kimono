BOTTOM LINE
------------
[V]  The INTx hypothesis holds at the driver level. With the NIC swapped to rtl8139, pcid
     spawns rtl8139d, the driver prints its banner WITH an interrupt spec, the kernel opens
     the DTB interrupt-map phandle IRQ (`open_phandle_irq virq=37`), and the driver then
     sits in its interrupt-read loop. No MSI-X panic, no "no interrupts supported at all",
     no "no legacy interrupts supported", no "failed to open IRQ file". This is the first

PANIC ATTRIBUTION (why pre_nic_panics=2 is not a NIC failure)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Every boot of this ISO panics in the DISK driver, exactly as in the recorded
docs/workbench/E1-REDOX-SERIAL.txt from the K14 era:

  161: pcid-spawner: spawn "/scheme/initfs/lib/drivers/virtio-blkd"
  162: virtio-blk: initiating startup sequence :^)
  165: not implemented: virtio: MSI-X is not implemented on this architecture
  166: [ERROR virtio-blkd@src/header/stdlib/mod.rs:121] Abort
  167: kernel::arch::aarch64::interrupt::exception:ERROR -- FATAL: Not an SVC induced synchronous exception (ty=111100)

That abort happens BEFORE rtl8139d is spawned, so it is counted in the `pre_nic_panics`
column (2 lines: the MSI-X stub panic + the FATAL) and excluded from the NIC verdict by
scoring the serial from the NIC driver's spawn onward (intx-attempt-N-nicscope.txt).
The disk panic is a separate, pre-existing defect and is NOT evidence for or against INTx.

In attempt 4 two further `FATAL: Not an SVC` lines appear AFTER the rtl8139d banner, inside
the NIC window. They are attributed to userspace, not to the driver — the kernel names the
dying process:

  kernel::context::signal:INFO -- UNHANDLED EXCEPTION, CPU #0, PID 40, NAME /usr/bin/ls, CONTEXT 0xfffffe8000152230
  kernel::context::signal:INFO -- UNHANDLED EXCEPTION, CPU #0, PID 42, NAME /usr/bin/ls, CONTEXT 0xfffffe8000152230

with ELR_EL1 0x0000000000C1D448 in the `ls` image, ESR_EL1 0xF2000001 and FAR_EL1
0x7fffffffae10 (an EL0 address). rtl8139d itself printed nothing after its BAR warning and
never aborted. The classifier therefore counts these as userspace crashes
(`fatal_unattributed = fatal − userspace_crash − virtio-blkd-abort = 2 − 2 − 0 = 0`).
This attribution rule is in the script, not a post-hoc excuse.
