## Paul Sajna's phone experiment

Paul Sajna's ["Redox in your pocket"](https://blog.paulsajna.com/redox-in-your-pocket/) is dated **2025-09-21**. It reports a **BlackBerry KEY2 LE** reaching a Redox login prompt through a Qualcomm U-Boot Android boot image and UEFI; this is a boot proof, not a OnePlus 6 driver proof. He used the `redox_desktop_aarch64_2025-09-21_91_redox-live.iso.zst` image, copied a phone DTB to the boot partition, and removed `pcid-spawner /etc/pcid.d/` from `/usr/lib/init.d/00_drivers` because PCI discovery halted this Qualcomm boot. Source: [Sajna, 2025-09-21](https://blog.paulsajna.com/redox-in-your-pocket/), steps 1–3; checked 2026-10-24.

The **exact published patch** is an inline diff in that [same post, lines 49–93](https://blog.paulsajna.com/redox-in-your-pocket/): `src/os/uefi/dtb.rs`, `get_dev_mem_region`, changes both `parent_bus_addr` and `addr_size` to `BE::read_u32(&chunk[0..4])` instead of using `address_cells`/`size_cells`. Sajna calls it a hack that "doesn't make a lot of sense" and says he had **not upstreamed** it as of **2025-09-21**. The patch text is available; a named branch, commit, and MR for it are **UNKNOWN**. Treat it as a reproduction clue and fix cell indexing from the DT specification and a captured phone DTB rather than applying the 32-bit substitution blindly. Source: [Sajna, 2025-09-21](https://blog.paulsajna.com/redox-in-your-pocket/); checked 2026-10-24.

His follow-up reports intermittent boot affected by the memory map and stops at login; touchscreen, storage, audio, Wi-Fi, and a OnePlus 6 boot are **UNKNOWN** from that report. Source: [Sajna, 2025-09-21](https://blog.paulsajna.com/redox-in-your-pocket/), end of post; checked 2026-10-24. His published procedure flashes/erases phone partitions, so it is **not** a safe literal runbook for this project's existing `fastboot boot` and backup gates. Source: [Sajna, 2025-09-21](https://blog.paulsajna.com/redox-in-your-pocket/), steps 1–2; local project constraint: [PLAN.md](./PLAN.md), read 2026-10-24.
